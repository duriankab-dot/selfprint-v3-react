# Protecting Your Health When Work Won't Slow Down

"Once this project is over" is a sentence most people say permanently, one project at a time. Work rarely slows down on its own — the boundary has to be built regardless of the workload, not after it.

## Why Waiting Doesn't Work

Busy periods create the belief that health can be paused temporarily. But one busy period is usually followed by another, and each pause makes the next one easier to justify — until "temporary" quietly becomes permanent.

## A Way Through It

Pick two or three health non-negotiables that survive any workload — a fixed sleep time, a lunch actually eaten away from your desk, one form of movement most days. Keep those fixed regardless of how busy work gets, and let everything else flex around them instead of the other way around.

The goal isn't balancing everything perfectly. It's protecting the two or three things that, if lost, cost you the most.

## Key Takeaways

- "Once things slow down" rarely arrives on its own
- Fix two or three non-negotiables instead of trying to balance everything
- Let the workload flex around your health minimums, not the reverse

## How Your Twin Guides You

Your Twin can flag when your actual behavior has quietly drifted from your stated non-negotiables — the gap that's easy to miss week to week but adds up over months.
