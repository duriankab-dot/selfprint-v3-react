# Pacing an Injury Recovery Without Losing Momentum

Two people get the same injury. One rushes back too soon and re-injures it. The other gets so cautious they never fully return. Both outcomes come from the same missing thing: a real pacing plan instead of a feeling-based one.

## Why "Listen to Your Body" Isn't Enough

Pain tolerance and motivation both distort how your body actually feels day to day — some days it feels worse than the injury actually is, some days motivation talks you into ignoring real warning signs. "Listen to your body" alone isn't a reliable enough signal on its own.

## A Way Through It

Use a percentage-based return instead of a feeling-based one: come back at roughly 50% of pre-injury intensity, hold there for a set number of sessions with no pain increase, then step up in small, planned increments — not in response to a good day.

This keeps momentum going (you're not waiting for "fully healed" to start anything) while removing the guesswork that causes both re-injury and over-caution.

## Key Takeaways

- "Listen to your body" is too noisy a signal to pace recovery alone
- A planned percentage-based return beats a feeling-based one
- Small, scheduled increments prevent both re-injury and stalling out
