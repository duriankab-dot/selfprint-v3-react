# Managing Caregiver Burnout

Caregiver burnout is uniquely hard to name, because admitting it can feel like a betrayal of the person you're caring for — as if your exhaustion is a complaint about them, rather than a real and separate cost of the role.

## Why It Goes Unspoken Longer Than Other Burnout

Other kinds of burnout have an obvious exit — quit the job, end the situation. Caregiving usually doesn't, especially for family. That absence of an exit makes people suppress the exhaustion rather than name it, since naming it feels pointless if nothing can change.

## A Way Through It

Separate the two questions that get merged: "do I love this person and want to care for them" and "am I currently depleted in a way that needs support." Both can be true at once — loving someone doesn't cancel out needing relief, even briefly, from the physical and emotional cost of caregiving.

Look specifically for small, real relief — a few hours covered by someone else, a support group, a single task delegated — rather than waiting for the situation itself to change.

## Key Takeaways

- Caregiver burnout is real even when love for the person is also real
- The lack of an obvious exit makes it easy to suppress rather than name
- Small, concrete relief matters more than waiting for the situation to change
