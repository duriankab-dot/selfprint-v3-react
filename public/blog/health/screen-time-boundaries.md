# Setting Screen Time Boundaries That Work

A screen time limit that just blocks an app rarely survives more than a few days, because it fights the pull directly instead of addressing what the pull is actually for.

## Why Blanket Limits Usually Fail

Reaching for a phone is rarely about the phone itself — it's boredom, avoidance, anxiety, or a habit loop looking for a quick hit of stimulation. A blocker removes the tool but leaves the underlying pull completely intact, so it either gets bypassed or transfers to a different app entirely.

## A Way Through It

Instead of blocking everything, target the specific moments the pull is strongest — right after waking, mid-afternoon slump, right before bed — and put one small friction point there specifically (phone charging outside the bedroom, one required extra tap to open the app). Friction at the right moment beats a blanket rule everywhere.

Pair it with one genuine alternative for that same moment, so the underlying need still gets met a different way.

## Key Takeaways

- Screen pulls are about an underlying need, not the app itself
- Target friction at your specific high-pull moments, not everywhere at once
- Pair removed screen time with a real alternative for that moment
