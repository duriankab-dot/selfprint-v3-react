# Making Time for Preventive Care

Preventive care competes against tasks with visible deadlines, and it loses almost every time — because nothing bad happens the day you skip a checkup. The cost only shows up later, disconnected from the decision that caused it.

## Why It Keeps Losing

Anything urgent has a deadline attached; preventive care doesn't. Without an artificial deadline, it drifts indefinitely, deprioritized by definition every single week, until something forces the issue.

## A Way Through It

Treat preventive appointments the way you'd treat a meeting with your own boss — schedule them at the start of the year for the whole year, and protect that slot the same way you'd protect any commitment you couldn't casually cancel.

The goal is removing the decision entirely: it's not "should I go this month," it's "I already have a slot booked."

## Key Takeaways

- Preventive care has no natural deadline, so it needs an artificial one
- Book the whole year's appointments at once, not one at a time
- Treat the slot as a fixed commitment, not a flexible one
