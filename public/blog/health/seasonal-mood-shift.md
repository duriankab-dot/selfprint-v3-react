# Handling the Seasonal Mood Shift

If your mood reliably dips at the same time every year, treating it as a surprise each time wastes the one advantage you actually have: you can see it coming.

## Why It Catches People Off Guard Anyway

Seasonal mood shifts are gradual, so by the time it's fully noticeable, it's already underway and harder to address from a depleted state. Most people only respond once they're already in it, rather than preparing while they're still in a better place to act.

## A Way Through It

Mark the date range on your calendar when the dip has hit in past years, and put your countermeasures in place a few weeks before it, not after: more deliberate light exposure, a maintained movement routine, protected social contact — the things most likely to slip first once the dip begins.

Treat it like preparing for a known weather pattern, not an unpredictable event.

## Key Takeaways

- A recurring seasonal dip is predictable, not a surprise each year
- Countermeasures work best started before the dip, not during it
- Protect the specific habits most likely to slip first (light, movement, contact)
