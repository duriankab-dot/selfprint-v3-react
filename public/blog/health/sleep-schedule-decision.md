# Fixing a Broken Sleep Schedule

You've tried going to bed earlier. It didn't work — you just lay there awake, then felt worse the next day for having "failed" at sleep on top of being tired.

## What's Actually Broken

A broken sleep schedule is rarely about willpower. It's usually one of three things: your wake time is inconsistent, your light exposure is backwards (dim mornings, bright screens at night), or your body genuinely doesn't feel tired at the hour you're trying to force sleep.

Trying to fix bedtime directly attacks the symptom, not the cause.

## The Lever That Actually Works

Fix your **wake time** first, not your bedtime. Get up at the same hour every day, including weekends, for two weeks — no matter how little you slept. Get bright light (ideally outside) within 30 minutes of waking.

This resets your circadian rhythm from the anchor point that's easiest to control. Bedtime naturally follows within a week or two, without you forcing it.

## Key Takeaways

- Consistent wake time fixes sleep schedules faster than consistent bedtime
- Morning light exposure is doing more work than you think
- Two weeks of consistency, not one night of trying harder
