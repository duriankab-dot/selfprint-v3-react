# Quitting a Habit That's Not Working For You Anymore

Most attempts to quit a habit attack the action directly — "I'll just stop." That fails more often than it works, because the action was never the point. It was solving something.

## What the Habit Is Actually For

Every persistent habit is meeting a real need, even an unhelpful one — stress relief, boredom relief, social connection, avoidance of a harder task. Quitting the action without replacing what it was solving just leaves the underlying need unmet, which is why willpower alone burns out.

## A Way Through It

Before trying to quit, spend a week just noticing: what happens right before you do the habit, and what do you feel right after. That reveals the actual trigger and the actual reward — the two things you need to replace, not just the middle action.

Then swap in a replacement that serves the same need through a different action, rather than trying to leave the need unmet.

## Key Takeaways

- The habit is solving a real need — quitting it cold ignores that need
- A week of noticing trigger and reward reveals what to actually replace
- Swap the action, not the need, for a change that actually holds
