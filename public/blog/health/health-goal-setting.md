# Setting Health Goals That Actually Stick

"Get healthier" fails before it starts, not because of low motivation, but because it isn't a decision anyone can actually act on tomorrow morning. Vague goals produce vague follow-through.

## Where Health Goals Actually Break

Most health goals are outcome-based ("lose weight," "get fit") rather than action-based ("walk 20 minutes after lunch"). Outcomes take weeks or months to show progress, so there's no daily signal telling you whether today counted — which makes it easy to quietly drift.

## A Way Through It

Set the goal at the level of a specific, repeatable action you can complete today, not an outcome you'll only see months from now. Track whether you did the action, not whether the outcome has arrived yet — the outcome follows the action eventually, on its own schedule.

Pick one action, not five, until it's automatic. Then add the next.

## Key Takeaways

- Outcome-based goals give no daily signal; action-based goals do
- Track the action you completed today, not the distant outcome
- One action at a time beats five actions abandoned together
