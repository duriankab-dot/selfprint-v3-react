# Managing Stress That Won't Go Away

Breathing exercises help with a stressful moment. They do almost nothing for stress that's been running for six months, because chronic stress isn't a spike — it's a baseline that's shifted.

## Why the Usual Tricks Stop Working

Techniques built for acute stress (a five-minute breathing exercise, a walk around the block) are aimed at bringing a spike back down. Chronic stress has no spike to bring down — it's already the resting state, so those tools have nothing to act on.

## A Way Through It

Instead of asking "how do I calm down," ask "what structural thing has been true for months that's keeping this baseline elevated" — a workload, a relationship, a financial situation, an unresolved decision. Chronic stress usually traces back to one or two structural sources, not a general mood.

Address the source directly, even partially, and techniques that felt useless before start working again — because now there's an actual spike to bring down instead of a permanent plateau.

## Key Takeaways

- Chronic stress is a shifted baseline, not a spike — acute-stress tools won't touch it
- Look for the one or two structural sources keeping the baseline elevated
- Fixing the source, even partly, makes ordinary coping tools effective again

Your Twin can flag when a stress pattern has been consistent for weeks rather than a one-off bad day — a useful distinction most people lose track of while they're inside it.
