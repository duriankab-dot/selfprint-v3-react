# Cutting Through Supplement Hype

A supplement aisle is designed to make every bottle look essential. Almost none of them are, and figuring out which ones actually matter takes about five minutes of the right questions — not a chemistry degree.

## The Filter That Actually Works

Ask three questions before buying anything: is there a documented deficiency you're actually correcting (a blood test, not a feeling), is the effective dose in this product close to what research actually used, and would the same money spent on food or sleep do more for the same goal.

Most supplements fail at least one of these three, which is usually enough to skip them.

## A Way Through It

Start from documented deficiency, not from a claim on the label. If a blood panel shows you're low in something specific, that's worth correcting deliberately. If you're supplementing because an ad implied you might be "running low" on something vague, that's marketing doing the deciding for you.

## Key Takeaways

- Documented deficiency beats vague marketing claims every time
- Check the actual dose against what research used, not just the ingredient name
- Compare against food and sleep as cheaper, often more effective alternatives
