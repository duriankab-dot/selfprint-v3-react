# Industry Switch: Is Your Twin Guiding You Right?

You've been in tech for 8 years. Now you want to move into education. Or finance into healthcare. Or consulting into creative work.

Industry switches feel scary because they are: You're leaving credibility in one domain to start as a junior in another.

Your Twin knows this decision is worth making sometimes—and worth *not* making other times.

## Why Industry Switches Fail

Most industry switches fail not because the new industry isn't good, but because people underestimate what they're giving up.

**What you lose when you switch industries:**

1. **Credibility** — You're no longer the expert. You're the newcomer.
2. **Network** — Your 8 years of relationships mean less in a new field.
3. **Compensation** — You'll often start at a lower level and salary.
4. **Speed** — What took you 2 years to learn in tech might take 3-4 in a new field.
5. **Advancement** — You go backward before you go forward.

People often underestimate this. They think: "I have 8 years of work ethic. I can apply that anywhere."

True, but it doesn't erase the cost.

## The Twin Framework: When to Switch Industries

### Filter 1: Are You Running Away or Running Toward?

**Running away:**
- "Tech burnout is real"
- "I hate my manager"
- "This industry is toxic"

**Running toward:**
- "Healthcare fascinates me"
- "I want to solve education problems"
- "This field aligns with my values"

If you're mostly running away, switching industries won't help. You'll bring the burnout with you.

**Rule:** Only switch industries if you're at least 60% running toward something, not away.

### Filter 2: Does This Switch Leverage Your Existing Skills?

You spent 8 years in tech. What skills transfer?

**Tech → EdTech:** Teaching experience + technical skills = valuable  
**Tech → FinTech:** Systems thinking + technology + compliance = valuable  
**Tech → Media:** Project management + understanding user experience = valuable  

Compare:

**Tech → Healthcare (non-tech role):** Limited transfer. You'd start over.  
**Tech → Nonprofit management:** Some transfer. Management skills matter.

The more your skills transfer, the easier the switch.

### Filter 3: Can You Actually Afford It?

Industry switches usually mean:
- Entry-level or junior compensation in new field
- Taking 6-12 months to learn domain basics
- Starting relationships from zero

Can you afford that financially? Do you have runway?

If you have $15k saved and need to take a $20k pay cut, that's not feasible. That's a blocker.

### Filter 4: Is This a Values Shift or a Skill Shift?

**Values shift:** "I want to work on problems that matter morally"  
This is worth the cost. Often worth a pay cut.

**Skill shift:** "Programming is boring, I want to design"  
This might be worth it. Depends on how much you want it.

**Escape:** "This industry is killing me"  
This is usually NOT worth it. Fix the burnout first.

## How to Switch Industries (Without Starting Over)

If you've passed those filters, here's how to switch without erasing your value:

### Strategy 1: Find the Bridge

Don't jump directly from Tech to EdTech. Find the bridge:

**Transition:** Tech → EdTech consulting → EdTech company

First, work for a consulting firm that serves education. You learn the industry while bringing technical expertise.

Then, you're "industry experienced" when you interview at an actual EdTech company.

### Strategy 2: Start With Contracting

Many industries hire contractors/consultants from outside.

You contract for 6 months in your new industry. Learn the domain. Build the network. Then transition to full-time with credibility.

### Strategy 3: Take a Hybrid Role

Look for roles that value your existing skills:

Instead of: "Product Manager (needs 5 years EdTech experience)"  
Look for: "Operations Manager at EdTech startup" (needs systems thinking + discipline)

You're using your existing skills while learning the industry.

## The Timing Question

When should you make the switch?

**Wait if:**
- You're burned out (fix first)
- You have no savings (build runway)
- You're early in your tech career (you haven't built enough expertise to transfer)

**Go for it if:**
- You have 5-10+ years of credibility in your current field
- You have 6-12 months of runway
- You've confirmed the new industry actually interests you (not just fantasy)
- You can find a bridge role or leverage existing skills

## Key Takeaways

- Industry switches cost credibility, network, and often compensation
- Only switch if running toward something, not away
- Look for ways your skills transfer to minimize the starting-over feeling
- Find a bridge role or hybrid position when possible
- Make sure you have runway and it's not an escape decision

## How Your Twin Guides You

Your Twin separates "I'm bored" from "I need to change industries." They look similar at first. They're very different decisions.

Sometimes the best move is a different role in the same industry. Sometimes it's a different industry entirely. Your Twin helps you know which.
