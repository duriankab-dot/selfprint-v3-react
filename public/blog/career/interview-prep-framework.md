# Interview Preparation: Decision-Making Framework

Interview nerves are usually about one thing: **You're afraid of being rejected.**

But here's what your Twin sees that you might not: **You're also interviewing them.**

Most people prepare for interviews by memorizing answers to common questions. "Tell me about a weakness." "Where do you see yourself in 5 years?" They practice their responses until they feel robotic.

Your Twin knows that's backwards. The real interview preparation is learning to *evaluate fit* while they're evaluating you.

## The Wrong Way to Prepare for an Interview

You research the company. You find the job description. You imagine questions and rehearse answers.

Then the interview happens, and you're in survival mode—trying to impress, trying not to make mistakes, hoping they like you.

You leave without any real sense of whether this job is actually good for *you*.

**That's a missed opportunity.**

## The Twin Framework: Interview as a Two-Way Evaluation

An interview isn't an audition. It's a mutual assessment. You're trying to understand:

- Will this role help you grow?
- Does the team work in a way that energizes you?
- Are the values aligned with yours?
- Will you still respect this decision in 6 months?

### Part 1: Before the Interview—Research Differently

Most people research company facts: "Founded in 2010, 500 employees, Series C funding."

Your Twin knows that misses the actual data. Here's what to actually research:

**1. The Person Interviewing You**
- Find them on LinkedIn. What's their background?
- How long have they been at the company? (Quick turnover is a red flag)
- What's their career path? (Are they growing *there*?)

**2. Company Culture Signals**
- Read Glassdoor reviews. Look for patterns, not just star ratings
- Search "[Company name] layoffs" or "[Company name] leadership changes"
- Look at their recent hires on LinkedIn—where do they come from?

**3. Role-Specific Reality**
- How many people have held this role in the past 2 years? (High turnover in one role is telling)
- What happened to the last person in this role? (Promoted? Left? Lateral move?)
- Who would you actually report to? (Find them—understand their management style)

**4. Growth Trajectory**
- How many job openings do they have open right now?
- Are they hiring across departments or just certain areas? (Healthy growth vs. desperation hiring)

This research takes an hour. It changes everything.

## Part 2: During the Interview—Ask the Right Questions

Most candidates ask safe questions:
- "What does a typical day look like?"
- "What are the growth opportunities?"
- "What's the company culture?"

These questions are fine. But they're not diagnostic.

Your Twin asks different questions—ones that reveal the real situation:

### Question 1: "What's the biggest challenge this role would face in the first 3 months?"

This isn't small talk. Their answer tells you:
- Do they have realistic expectations?
- Are there known problems they're being honest about?
- What would you actually be solving?

A good answer: "We need to integrate three data systems—it's technically complex and politically complicated."

A bad answer: "Nothing really, it's pretty smooth sailing."

### Question 2: "What did the previous person in this role do well?"

This signals whether:
- They've reflected on the role
- They have constructive feedback
- They actually know what success looks like

### Question 3: "How does this company support people who want to grow?"

Listen for:
- Specific examples, not platitudes
- Whether they mention people who've been promoted from within
- Their own growth story at this company

People who've grown at the company know it. People in stagnant roles can't articulate growth.

### Question 4: "If I were to ask three people on the team what it's like to work here, what would they say?"

This is the revealing question. Are they proud? Do they worry about what people would say? This question cuts through rehearsed messaging.

### Question 5: "What does success look like for this role? How would you measure it?"

Their answer tells you:
- Are expectations clear or vague?
- Is it a 6-month eval or a 2-year vision?
- Will you have autonomy or constant oversight?

## Part 3: After the Interview—Your Twin's Evaluation

You got the job offer. Now comes the real decision—and this is where your Twin matters most.

Create an assessment matrix:

```
ROLE: Senior Developer at TechCorp

FIT FACTORS (Rate 1-10)
Technical Growth:        8   (New stack, good mentorship)
Compensation:            7   (Market rate + equity)
Work-Life Balance:       6   (60-hour weeks common)
Team Fit:                8   (Collaborative, inclusive)
Company Stability:       6   (Series B, 18 months runway)
Alignment w/ Values:     7   (Product matters, impact real)
Opportunity to Lead:     5   (Unlikely in first year)
Geographic/Lifestyle:    9   (Remote, where I want to be)

OVERALL:                 7/10 (Good but not perfect)

REVERSIBILITY:
This decision IS reversible. If it doesn't work, I can 
leave in 12 months and go elsewhere. That gives me permission 
to take this bet.

RED FLAGS TO MONITOR:
- Series B runway is tight (watch for layoff signals)
- Work-life balance is concerning (can I negotiate this?)
- Leadership opportunity is limited (is this a stepping stone only?)

QUESTIONS TO NEGOTIATE:
Before accepting, clarify:
- Work hours expectations?
- Leadership/mentorship opportunities in Year 2?
- Learning budget?
```

This matrix isn't about perfect scores. It's about clarity. You're not trying to find a perfect job—you're trying to understand what you're actually choosing.

## Key Takeaways

- Interview prep is half about impressing them, half about evaluating fit for yourself
- Research *people* and *patterns*, not just facts
- Ask diagnostic questions that reveal the real situation
- After an offer, create a decision matrix to evaluate the actual opportunity
- Accept offers where the tradeoffs make sense for *your* next 2-3 years

## How Your Twin Guides You

Your Twin's role in interview season is to keep you from making decisions in desperation or vanity.

Sometimes the best offer isn't the right opportunity. Sometimes the smaller role at a better company is smarter than the senior title at a sinking ship.

Twin guidance helps you see past the immediate pressure to land the job, so you can actually land the *right* job.
