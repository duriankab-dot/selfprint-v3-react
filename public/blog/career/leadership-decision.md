# Leadership Role Decision: Are You Ready?

The offer came: Team lead. People manager. Leadership track.

You want it. You're also terrified.

Leadership means:
- Responsibility for people's careers (not just tasks)
- Hard conversations (underperformance, firing)
- Your mistakes affect others
- You're no longer an individual contributor

Your Twin knows whether you're ready—and what readiness actually means.

## What Changes When You Become a Leader

**You stop doing the work yourself.**

This is both liberation and grief. You were good at your work. Now someone else does it.

**Your job becomes enabling others.**

Instead of shipping features, you unblock people from shipping features. It's indirect.

**You're accountable for others.**

Their success becomes your success. Their failure is on you. That's heavy.

**Politics become real.**

As IC, you could be apolitical. As leader, you have to navigate politics, negotiate resources, navigate disagreements.

**Your time is no longer yours.**

1-on-1s, team meetings, organizational meetings. Your calendar fills. Deep work becomes rare.

## The Twin Framework: Are You Ready?

### Readiness Filter 1: Can You Stop Being the Expert?

This is the hardest part for strong individual contributors.

You were the person everyone came to with hard problems. Now your team members need to figure it out.

**Question:** Can you be comfortable not being the smartest person solving the problem?

If no → You'll have hard time as leader. You'll either micromanage or be absent trying to do IC work.

If yes → Continue.

### Readiness Filter 2: Do You Actually Want to Manage People?

Not: "Does it look good?" or "Is it promotion?"

**Real question:** Do you enjoy helping people grow and solve problems?

Some people like leadership because it's a title. They actually hate people management.

**Real tell:** When someone comes to you with a problem, do you light up or dread it?

If dread → Consider alternative leadership (architect, principal) that doesn't involve people management.

If excitement → You might genuinely want this.

### Readiness Filter 3: Can You Have Hard Conversations?

Leadership requires uncomfortable conversations:

- "Your performance isn't good enough"
- "You're not ready for promotion yet"
- "I'm not hiring you for this team"
- "You need to improve or leave"

If you avoid conflict, leadership will be agony.

**Real tell:** Think of a time you had to give someone hard feedback. How did that go? Did you do it directly or avoid it?

### Readiness Filter 4: Are You Ready to Be Politically Aware?

Leadership means navigating organizational politics.

- Advocating for your team's resources
- Managing up (your manager)
- Collaborating with peer leaders
- Explaining decisions to people who disagree

If politics drain you, consider carefully. Leadership has more politics.

### Readiness Filter 5: Can You Handle Responsibility for Failure?

As IC, failures are shared or small-scoped. As leader, your team's failures are your responsibility.

**Question:** Can you own failures and learn from them without spiraling?

## Red Flags That You're Not Ready

**Red Flag 1:** You want leadership to escape your current role, not to lead people

(If your job is unbearable, fix that first)

**Red Flag 2:** You've never given hard feedback to a peer or managed anyone

(Get some experience first)

**Red Flag 3:** You don't know what good leadership looks like

(Learn from mentors before accepting role)

**Red Flag 4:** You're burned out

(Leadership adds stress, not relief)

**Red Flag 5:** You believe being smart is enough

(Leadership is about enabling others, not being smartest)

## Green Flags That You Might Be Ready

**Green Flag 1:** You've mentored people successfully

(They grew. They trusted you. You enjoyed it)

**Green Flag 2:** You've led projects with clarity and decisiveness

(People knew the direction. Work got done)

**Green Flag 3:** You enjoy coaching and problem-solving with people

(Not *at* them. *With* them)

**Green Flag 4:** You can take feedback and admit when wrong

(Leaders need humility)

**Green Flag 5:** You're hungry to grow as a leader, not just collect titles

(You know it's hard. You want to learn)

## The First Year Will Be Hard

**Expect:**
- Imposter syndrome
- Failure and learning
- Relationships changing
- Exhaustion unlike IC role
- Questions about your capability

**This is normal.** Most leaders go through this.

**Get support:** Find a leadership mentor or coach. Read about leadership. Build your own leadership philosophy.

## Key Takeaways

- Leadership means enabling others, not doing the work
- Real readiness: You want to manage people, not just get promoted
- You need to be comfortable having hard conversations
- You need some leadership experience before taking first role
- First year will be hard; that's normal

## How Your Twin Guides You

Your Twin separates the title from the reality.

Sometimes the right move is senior IC role, not leadership. Some people thrive most as individual contributors.

Don't take the leadership role just because it's offered. Take it because you genuinely want to develop others.
