# How to Evaluate Job Offers Like Your Twin Would

You've received an offer. Exciting. Terrifying. Real.

Now comes the part nobody teaches you: how to actually *evaluate* it. Because saying "yes" to a job offer is one of the biggest decisions you'll make that year—and most people make it in a rush, comparing mostly salary numbers.

Your Twin thinks differently about job offers. It looks beyond the paycheck to see what this role will *actually* cost you and give you.

## The Hidden Layers of a Job Offer

Every job offer is a package deal:
- **Visible:** Salary, title, benefits, location
- **Semi-visible:** Team quality, boss's reputation, company growth
- **Hidden:** Company culture, learning opportunity, career trajectory, life impact

Most people optimize for what's visible. Your Twin reveals the hidden layers.

## The Twin Evaluation Framework

When evaluating a job offer, ask these five questions in order:

### Question 1: Does This Role Offer What You Actually Need Right Now?

Not what *sounds* good. What you actually need.

If you're burned out, a 30% raise at a company with worse work-life balance is a trap. If you're trying to learn a new skill, a high-paying role where you'll just do what you already know is expensive in disguise.

**Honest inventory:**
- What's broken in your current situation?
- What would fix it?
- Does this offer fix it, or just move the problem?

Example: Alex was offered a director role at a bigger company. More title, more money. But he realized his real problem wasn't needing more authority—it was needing to master a technical skill before going into management. He turned down the director role and took an IC (individual contributor) role instead, buying himself 2 years to deepen expertise.

### Question 2: Who Will You Be Working For?

Your manager is 80% of your job satisfaction. A great manager at a mediocre company beats a bad manager at a dream company.

**What to investigate:**
- How long has this manager been at the company?
- How many people have they managed before?
- What do their former reports say? (LinkedIn, ask around)
- How often do they give feedback?
- Do they advocate for their team?

Serious question: would you want your manager as a mentor? If the answer is "not really," the offer gets weaker regardless of salary.

### Question 3: Can You Actually Do This Job?

Not "can you learn to do it," but "do you have 70%+ of the skills they're asking for?"

If you're 40% there, you'll spend your first year fighting uphill. If you're 70%+ there, you can contribute quickly and learn the rest on the job.

This isn't about false modesty. It's about honesty. If the offer is exciting *because* it's beyond your capabilities, that's thrilling. But if it's exciting *despite* being beyond your capabilities, that's a yellow flag.

### Question 4: Will You Respect Yourself in This Role?

Some offers are objectively good but ethically problematic. 

- Will you respect the company's business practices?
- Will you be proud to tell people what you do there?
- Are there values conflicts you're overlooking because the offer is shiny?

This matters more than you think. Career regret isn't always about the role failing—it's about compromising on values for money.

### Question 5: What's the Real Salary After Costs?

This is where Twin math differs from surface-level thinking.

If the new job is 100km away and you'll spend 3 hours commuting daily, that's 15 hours weekly. At your hourly rate, that's a pay cut you don't see on the offer letter.

**Calculate true hourly value:**
```
Salary: $120,000
+ Benefits value: $10,000
+ Commute time cost: -$15,000/year
+ Relocation hassle: -$5,000
+ Reduced personal time value: ???

Effective value ≠ What they're offering
```

Some offers look great on paper, then turn out to pay much less when you account for commute, relocation, or time investment.

## The Decision Moment

You've asked all five questions. Now comes the hardest part: **deciding what matters most**.

Some people would take a lower salary for a better mentor. Others would take a longer commute for a role that advances their five-year goal. There's no universal "right" answer.

But here's what matters: **decide consciously**.

Not "this is the only offer I'll get so I have to take it," but "knowing all of this, here's why this aligns with what I actually want."

## Key Takeaways

- **Don't just compare salaries.** Evaluate the full package: manager, team, growth, alignment.
- **Be honest about what you need right now.** Not next year. Now.
- **Calculate the real cost.** Commute, relocation, and opportunity cost matter.
- **Your manager matters more than the title.** Choose the better boss.
- **Honor your values.** Career regret often comes from ignoring them.
