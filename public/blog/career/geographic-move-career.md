# Geographic Move for Career: Twin Perspective

The job offer came. It's in San Francisco. You're in Columbus.

The opportunity is real. The salary is compelling. The career growth is clear.

But you have a life in Columbus. Family. Friends. Roots.

**Your Twin knows this decision is bigger than career opportunity.**

## The Math (That Matters Less Than It Seems)

People focus on salary:

"$120k in San Francisco vs. $90k in Columbus... let me calculate cost of living..."

This is backwards. The financial calculation is easiest part.

**Real questions:**
- Will you be happy in the new city?
- Are you moving toward opportunity or away from problems?
- Can your relationships survive relocation?
- Do you have runway to adjust if it doesn't work?

## The Twin Framework: Geographic Move Decisions

### Filter 1: Why Are You Moving?

**Moving toward opportunity:** "This role doesn't exist in my city"  
**Moving away from problem:** "I need to escape this place"

These are different decisions.

**Moving toward is higher likelihood of success.** You have a specific goal pulling you.

**Moving away often disappoints.** The problem isn't the city. It's something internal.

(If it's the city that's the problem, move. But fix the other first.)

### Filter 2: Does This Move Advance Your Actual Career?

**Question:** Would you take this job without the relocation?

If no → The relocation isn't the advantage. The fresh start is. That might wear off.

If yes → The opportunity is real. The location is secondary.

### Filter 3: How Important Are Your Existing Relationships?

**Be honest:**
- Do you have deep friendships here? (That matter to you)
- Do you have family obligations? (That would suffer)
- Do you have a partner? (Whose career also matters)
- Do you have community? (That supports your wellbeing)

**The impact:**
- Leaving established relationships is real cost
- Building new relationships takes 2-3 years
- Remote relationships with close people are hard
- Relocation affects not just career, but whole life

### Filter 4: Have You Visited the New City?

**Important:** Visit during work week (not just weekend).

Walk around after work. Eat dinner. Walk home. Check out neighborhoods. Feel the city when you'd actually be living there.

Does it feel like home? Does it feel exciting? Does it feel alienating?

Your gut matters here.

### Filter 5: Is This Reversible?

**Question:** Could you move back if this doesn't work?

- Leaving San Francisco, you could come back to tech anywhere
- Leaving Columbus, would your old job be available? Would your network wait?
- If this city doesn't work, can you find similar opportunity elsewhere?

More reversibility = safer to move.

## The Real Costs of Geographic Moves

**Financial:** Cost of living, moving expenses, housing adjustment

**Relational:** Leaving friends, family time, established community

**Emotional:** Adaptation period, homesickness, identity shift

**Career:** You might not grow as fast as you thought; might get isolated in new company

**Opportunity:** You lose access to local opportunities you had

**Reversibility:** Some moves are easy to reverse; some aren't

This isn't to say "don't move." It's to say: **Factor these in.**

## When Geographic Moves Make Sense

**Move if:**
- The career opportunity is genuinely rare (can't find it locally)
- Your relationships can handle it (partner/family on board, or you're okay being far away)
- You've visited and feel energized
- You have runway (savings, could return if needed)
- You're moving toward something, not away

**Don't move if:**
- You're just trying to escape
- The opportunity exists in your current city too
- Your key relationships would suffer
- You don't have financial runway
- You're burned out (moving won't fix that)

## How to Make It Work (If You Move)

**Before moving:**
- Take the job contingent on visiting the city
- Visit for 2-3 days in work week (not weekend fantasy)
- Talk to people who work at company (offline)
- Make sure partner/family is genuinely on board

**After moving:**
- Don't hide in apartment/work. Build community intentionally.
- Join a group/class related to your interest
- Schedule regular calls with people back home (don't let relationships atrophy)
- Give yourself 6-12 months to adjust (it takes time)
- Check in after 1-2 years: Is this working?

## Key Takeaways

- Geographic moves are bigger than career decisions
- Move toward opportunity, not away from problems
- Relationship impact matters (factor it in)
- Visit the city during work week before deciding
- Moves are easier to make than to reverse

## How Your Twin Guides You

Your Twin helps you see that relocating for career isn't just a spreadsheet calculation.

It's a life decision that affects your wellbeing, relationships, and identity.

The best geographic moves are ones where career opportunity + city + relationships all align.
