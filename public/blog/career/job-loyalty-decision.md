# Staying vs Leaving a Job You Love

This is the hardest career decision: You genuinely like your job. The team is amazing, the manager trusts you, the mission matters.

But you're not growing anymore.

You've mastered the skills. The projects feel familiar. The next 2 years look identical to the last 2 years.

**Do you stay in comfort or chase growth?**

Your Twin knows this decision determines how fulfilled you'll feel in 5 years.

## Why This Decision is Hard

Leaving a job you love feels like betrayal—of your manager, your team, your loyalty.

But staying in a job you've outgrown also hurts. It's slow stagnation.

Most people don't realize this tension until they've wasted years being comfortable instead of growing.

## The Twin Framework: Staying vs. Leaving

### Filter 1: Are You Actually Plateaued?

**Question:** Have you truly mastered your current role?

If the answer is **no**, you might just be experiencing burnout. It feels like stagnation but it's exhaustion.

Try this first:
- Take 2 weeks off
- Reduce project load
- Rediscover why the work mattered

If growth returns → Stay. You just needed a reset.

If nothing changes → You are actually plateaued. Continue to Filter 2.

### Filter 2: Are Growth Opportunities Hidden?

**Question:** Could you grow in different *directions* at this company?

You've mastered the IC role. But could you:
- Move into mentorship/leadership?
- Take on architectural decisions?
- Lead a cross-team initiative?
- Work on the company's hardest problems?
- Specialize deeper in your domain?

If **yes** → Have the conversation with your manager first. "I love it here, but I'm looking for new challenges. What's possible?"

Often managers don't know you're looking to grow. A honest conversation creates options.

If **no** → The company can't grow you more. Continue to Filter 3.

### Filter 3: What Will You Regret?

**The question your Twin really asks:** In 5 years, will you regret leaving or staying?

**Regret leaving if:**
- The team bonds become irreplaceable and you won't find that again
- The mission is genuinely unique (you'd be proud to have been there)
- Your manager is a rarity (great mentors are rare)
- The learning curve elsewhere would feel backstepped

**Regret staying if:**
- You realize you had skills others would have paid 30% more for
- You missed years developing expertise
- You stayed loyal to a company that would replace you in 2 weeks
- You chose comfort over becoming who you wanted to become

Which regret is bigger?

## The Financial Reality

Job loyalty is a luxury. Before you stay at a job you love for less pay, ask:

- **What's the market rate for my skills elsewhere?**
- **Am I leaving 20-30% compensation on the table?**
- **Over 10 years, how much loyalty costs me?**

$80k job you love vs. $100k job elsewhere = $200k difference over 10 years.

That's not nothing. That's house down payment, retirement funding, life flexibility money.

**Your Twin's principle:** You can't eat fulfillment. Compensation matters.

## When to Definitely Leave

**Leave if:**
- You've outgrown this role and growth isn't available
- You're taking 15-20% below market rate
- You're staying only out of guilt
- The company is declining (you see warning signs)
- Your manager changed and the new one is worse

## When to Definitely Stay

**Stay if:**
- Growth opportunities genuinely exist and you're pursuing them
- You're at market rate or above
- The team is unique and you're still learning from them
- The mission matters to your identity
- Staying is a choice, not fear of change

## The Decision Script

If you decide to leave:

**To yourself:** "I'm grateful for what I learned here. I'm ready for growth I can't find here. That's not disloyal—that's honoring my own development."

**To your manager:** "I've loved my time here. I've decided to pursue a new opportunity that will stretch me in ways this role doesn't. I appreciate you believing in me."

**When transitioning:** Do it professionally. Leave documentation. Train your replacement. Maintain the relationship.

The goal: Leave on good terms so they'd hire you back or refer you forward.

## Key Takeaways

- Plateauing in a job you love is real, and ignoring it costs years
- Before leaving, check if growth exists in different directions
- Regret is the best decision-maker here (what would you regret more?)
- Compensation matters more than we want to admit
- Leave well and maintain relationships

## How Your Twin Guides You

Your Twin helps you see that staying in comfort and chasing growth aren't mutually exclusive forever. There's a time to be loyal and a time to level up.

Both decisions are valid. The key is making them intentionally, not accidentally.
