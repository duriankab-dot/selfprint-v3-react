# Career Burnout: Time for a Change?

You're exhausted. Not just tired—exhausted at a cellular level. You dread Mondays. Work used to energize you, now it drains you.

You think: **I need to quit.**

But your Twin knows that burnout is a messenger, not a destination. It's telling you something needs to change—but that something might not be your job.

## What Burnout Actually Means

Burnout isn't laziness. It's not weakness. It's your system telling you something is unsustainable.

But what, exactly?

- Unsustainable hours?
- Unsustainable pressure?
- Unsustainable lack of growth?
- Unsustainable mismatch between your values and the work?
- Unsustainable relationship with your manager?
- Unsustainable lack of autonomy?

Each requires a different fix.

**Unsustainable hours** → Negotiate schedule or leave  
**Unsustainable growth** → Change teams or roles  
**Unsustainable values mismatch** → Leave  
**Unsustainable manager** → Transfer or leave  

See the difference? Three of those fixes don't require quitting.

## The Burnout Decision Tree

### Level 1: Is This Job Sustainable With Changes?

**Question:** Could you continue this work if the manager changed or hours decreased?

If YES → Try to fix it first (negotiate, transfer, change roles)  
If NO → This job isn't fixable. Start looking.

### Level 2: Is This Field Sustainable?

**Question:** If you left this job for another similar job, would the burnout return?

If NO → The job is the problem. Find a different company in the same field.  
If MAYBE → You might be burned out on the field entirely.

### Level 3: Do You Need a Pause, or a New Direction?

**Question:** If you had 6 months of sabbatical right now, would you want to come back?

If YES → You need rest + boundaries, not necessarily a new career  
If NO → You might need a change of direction

## The Twin Framework: Before You Quit

Before you resign, try this diagnostic:

### Step 1: Determine What Changed

When were you not burned out? Something was different then.

- You had a different manager
- You worked fewer hours
- The project was more interesting
- You had autonomy
- You felt valued
- You were learning

Write down: **What specifically changed?**

This tells you whether the fix is in or out of your control.

### Step 2: Can You Fix It Without Leaving?

**If it's your manager:**
- Can you transfer teams?
- Can you ask HR to involve them?
- Is this manager temporary?

**If it's hours:**
- Can you negotiate flex time?
- Can you take fewer projects?
- Can you delegate?

**If it's lack of growth:**
- Can you change roles internally?
- Can you get additional responsibility?
- Can you get mentorship?

**If it's values mismatch:**
- Are you actually in a values conflict, or just burned out?
- (Burnout distorts values perspective)

### Step 3: The 30-60-90 Day Experiment

Don't quit yet. Try a 30-60-90 day fix:

**Days 1-30: Set Boundaries**
- Leave at 5pm (even if work remains)
- Take full weekends off
- Don't check email after hours
- Take your vacation days

**Days 31-60: Fix What You Can**
- Have the manager conversation
- Propose the role change
- Request the transfer
- Ask for mentorship

**Days 61-90: Evaluate**
- Has your energy returned? (Boundary change works)
- Have conditions improved? (Action worked)
- Are you still burned out? (This job can't be fixed)

If you're still burned out after a genuine 90-day effort, now it's time to leave.

## When Quitting is the Right Answer

Quit if:

- You've tried boundary changes and they didn't help
- You've attempted fixes and management resisted
- The values mismatch is fundamental (you can't do this work in good conscience)
- Your health is genuinely at risk (not just tired, but physically unwell)
- The burnout has infected other areas (relationships, health, identity)

## How to Leave Well

If you decide to quit:

1. **Don't make the announcement in burnout.**
   Wait until you're clear-headed and have a plan.

2. **Secure finances.**
   Save 6-12 months of runway if possible.

3. **Have a plan for what's next.**
   Even if it's "take 2 months off, then explore," have a plan.

4. **Give proper notice.**
   Two weeks minimum (more if possible).

5. **Do the exit well.**
   Document your work, train your replacement, don't burn bridges.

You might return to this industry. Make leaving professional.

## Key Takeaways

- Burnout is a messenger, not a destiny
- Diagnose what's unsustainable (hours, growth, values, manager)
- Some fixes don't require quitting
- Try a 30-60-90 day boundary/fix experiment before resigning
- Only quit if the job can't be fixed from within

Sometimes the right answer is to stay and fix what's fixable. Sometimes it's to leave and find better. Your Twin helps you know which.
