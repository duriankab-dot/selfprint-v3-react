# Company Culture Fit: Red Flags and Green Lights

Culture is the water you swim in at work. You can have a great role at a toxic company, or a mediocre role at a thriving company.

The culture determines whether you stay energized or slowly drain.

Your Twin knows how to read culture before you're trapped in it.

## Why Culture Matters More Than Title

You accepted a senior role at a company with terrible culture.

6 months later: You're managing people but they're all leaving. The leadership is unreliable. The pace is unsustainable.

3 years in your dream title feels like years in purgatory.

**Culture isn't secondary. It's primary.**

Your title might be amazing. Your compensation might be great. If the culture sucks, you lose years of your life.

## Red Flag Culture (Signs to Run)

### Red Flag 1: High Turnover in Your Department

Check Glassdoor. Look at LinkedIn. How many people have left this team in the past year?

- 10% turnover = normal
- 25%+ turnover = run

**What it means:** People don't stay. Either the role is bad or the team is bad.

### Red Flag 2: Interviewers Seem Unhappy or Checked Out

You're interviewing with the team. They seem tired, cynical, or resentful.

**What it means:** These are your future colleagues. If they're unhappy, you will be too.

### Red Flag 3: No Clear Boundaries Between Work and Personal

They talk about work/life balance but then joke about working weekends.

"We're a startup, we all pitch in!" (Which means: nobody says no)

"We're a family here!" (Which means: work drama bleeds into relationships)

**What it means:** Boundaries are myth. You'll burn out.

### Red Flag 4: Ambiguous Expectations

They can't clearly define what success looks like. Goals keep changing.

"You'll know it when you see it"  
"We figure it out as we go"  
"It's fast-paced so nothing's set"

**What it means:** You'll work hard and never know if you're succeeding.

### Red Flag 5: Decisions Come From Opaque Top-Down

You don't understand how decisions are made. Direction changes without explanation.

"Upper management decided"  
"It comes from above"  
"That's just how it is"

**What it means:** You have no agency. You're just executing someone else's agenda.

### Red Flag 6: They Hire for "Culture Fit"

If "culture fit" is code for "we hire people like us," that's a red flag.

**What it means:** Conformity is valued over competence. Diverse thinking isn't welcome.

## Green Flag Culture (Signs to Seek)

### Green Flag 1: People Stay

Tenured team members. People who've been here 3-5+ years. Still excited.

**What it means:** It's actually good here.

### Green Flag 2: Interviewers Give Honest Answers

You ask: "What's hardest about working here?"

They give real answers: "Communication between teams is messy" or "We're growing faster than we can onboard people."

Not: "There's nothing hard!" (That's a lie)

**What it means:** They're honest. Culture is real, not marketing.

### Green Flag 3: Clear Boundaries

People leave at 5pm. They take vacation. They don't answer emails at midnight.

When you ask, "What's expected?", you get clear answer.

**What it means:** Sustainability is valued. They don't burn people out.

### Green Flag 4: Clear Success Criteria

You understand how your performance is measured.

- Quarterly goals with clear metrics
- Regular feedback (not surprise feedback)
- Path to next level is documented

**What it means:** You can see your progress. You're not guessing.

### Green Flag 5: Decisions Are Made Openly

People understand why decisions are made.

"We decided to pivot because..." (explanation given)  
"Here's how that decision was made..." (process is transparent)  

**What it means:** Trust exists. People know the direction.

### Green Flag 6: Diversity and Inclusion are Real, Not Performed

Not "we have a DEI committee" (and it does nothing).

But: People from different backgrounds, thinking styles, experience levels. All contributing.

**What it means:** Culture is actually inclusive, not performative.

## How to Assess Culture

**Before Interview:**
- Check Glassdoor reviews (patterns matter more than single reviews)
- Look at LinkedIn: Who's leaving? Who's staying?
- Ask your network: Anyone know people there?

**During Interview:**
- Ask one person: "What's the hardest part of working here?"
- Ask another: "Why have you stayed here for X years?"
- Notice: Do people seem energized or just collected?

**After Interview:**
- Did you feel excited or drained?
- Could you imagine working there daily?
- Did they answer your questions directly or dodge?

**If You Get Offer:**
- Ask to speak with someone who's been there 3+ years
- Ask: "What surprised you about the culture?"
- Trust your gut. If something felt off, it probably was

## When to Leave Due to Culture

You don't have to stay if culture is bad:

- Toxic leadership that won't change
- Burnout pace that isn't negotiable
- Exclusionary culture that makes you uncomfortable
- Dishonesty/lying at leadership level
- Pressure to compromise your values

Your Twin's principle: Your mental health matters more than the title.

## Key Takeaways

- Culture determines happiness more than title or pay
- High turnover is the biggest red flag
- Honest answers during interviews matter
- Clear boundaries and expectations signal healthy culture
- Assess culture through patterns, not single data points
