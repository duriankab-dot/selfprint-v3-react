# Networking Decisions: Building Your Career

Most people hate networking. The forced small talk, the transactional conversations, the anxiety of "selling yourself."

Your Twin knows something different: **Real networking is just authentic relationship-building at scale.**

The difference? Real networking doesn't feel like networking.

## Why Networking Matters (More Than You Think)

Research consistently shows: 70-85% of jobs are filled through networks, not job boards.

That's not because there's a conspiracy. It's because:
- Hiring managers trust referrals more than resumes
- Known candidates are lower risk
- Relationships matter when you're making major decisions

This isn't cynical. It's just how humans work. We trust people we know.

Your network determines which opportunities you even see. If nobody in your network knows about a job, you won't apply for it.

## The Networking Problem

Most networking advice sounds like this:
- "Attend 3 conferences per year"
- "Connect with 50 new people quarterly"
- "Follow up every month"

That sounds exhausting because it is. And most people don't sustain it.

**Your Twin's principle:** A smaller network of genuine relationships beats a huge network of hollow connections.

## The Twin Framework: Strategic Relationship Building

### Layer 1: Your Core Network (15-25 People)

These are people you actually care about. Not obligatory. Not transactional.

Who are they?
- Former colleagues you'd work with again
- People you learn from
- People who make you excited to connect
- People in your field or adjacent fields

Frequency: Monthly or quarterly touchpoints (real conversations, not obligation)

### Layer 2: Your Active Network (50-100 People)

These are people you know somewhat well. You engage when you're at events or when something relevant comes up.

Who are they?
- LinkedIn connections who are actually engaged
- People at your company you respect
- People in your industry you've met
- Friends of friends who share interests

Frequency: Whenever you see them, plus occasional thoughtful outreach

### Layer 3: Your Ambient Network (Everyone Else)

LinkedIn followers, Twitter connections, people who know your work.

These people don't require maintenance. They exist because you're visible in your field.

Frequency: No maintenance needed. Just be visible (share insights, contribute to conversations)

## The Decision Framework: Who to Invest In

Not everyone deserves equal networking energy. Decide strategically:

**Ask yourself about someone:**

1. **Do I genuinely like this person?**
   No = don't force it. Networking should feel good, not like work.

2. **Can we help each other?**
   (Not transactional, but mutual benefit should exist)
   Yes = invest

3. **Do they keep commitments?**
   (Flaky people drain energy)
   Yes = invest

4. **Am I energized or drained after talking to them?**
   Energized = core network candidate
   Drained = don't over-invest

If most answers are yes, move them to your core network.

## How to Actually Build Relationships

**The authentic way:**

1. **Show up (occasionally)**
   Attend conferences, meetups, or events related to your interest—not because you "should network," but because the event genuinely interests you.

2. **Be useful**
   Share insights. Make introductions. Help someone without expecting reciprocal help.
   People remember who helped them.

3. **Follow up genuinely**
   After meeting someone: "I really enjoyed our conversation about X. I found this article you might like."
   That's genuine follow-up. It takes 2 minutes.

4. **Stay visible**
   Share your work. Write posts. Contribute to discussions in your field.
   This isn't self-promotion. It's just existing loudly enough that people notice you.

5. **Make introductions**
   Know two people who should meet? Introduce them.
   This is the highest-value networking activity. You become known as a connector.

## The Introversion-Friendly Networking Approach

If you hate events, here's permission to build a network that works for you:

- **One-on-one coffee** > Room of 100 people
- **Thoughtful email** > Forced conference chat
- **Online communities** > In-person events
- **Deep relationships** > Hundreds of shallow ones

Introverts often have higher-quality networks because they invest in depth over breadth.

## Timing: When to Activate Your Network

Don't use your network only when you need something.

**Throughout your career:**
- Monthly: Grab coffee with someone in your core network
- Quarterly: Attend one event or make 5 meaningful outreach emails
- Annually: Have coffee with 6-12 people in your industry

**When you actually need something:**
- Job search, advice, introduction, referral
- Because you've invested in relationships, people actually help

The difference: You're calling in on goodwill you've built, not appearing out of nowhere asking for favors.

## The One Networking Mistake to Avoid

You add someone on LinkedIn and immediately send: "Hey, I'd love to pick your brain about X."

They don't know you. They ignore it.

**Better approach:**
1. Add them with a note: "I've followed your work on X for years. Would love to connect."
2. Wait for them to accept
3. Engage with their content for a few weeks
4. Then: "Your recent post on X resonated. I'm working on similar problem. Would love a quick call sometime."

Now they know you *before* you ask for their time.

## Key Takeaways

- Networking is relationship-building, not transaction
- Focus on depth (core network of 15-25) over breadth
- Be strategic: invest in people you like and who keep commitments
- Show up, be useful, follow up, stay visible
- Activate your network *before* you need it

Your Twin helps you see networking as a series of small decisions—about who to invest in, how often to connect, and when to ask for help.

The best networkers don't feel like networkers. They're just genuinely interested in people and skilled at maintaining relationships.
