# Commitment Conversations: When and How

You've been seeing someone for 2-3 months. It's going well. But neither of you has said "we're exclusive" or "this is a relationship."

The uncertainty is growing. You want to know where you stand. But asking feels risky.

**Your Twin knows this conversation is necessary, not risky.**

## Why People Avoid This Conversation

People fear:
- "They'll think I'm desperate"
- "They'll run away"
- "I'll seem too forward"
- "I'll ruin the vibe"

**None of this is true.**

A person worth being with wants clarity about commitment. Avoiding the conversation doesn't protect the relationship. It just creates more uncertainty.

## The Twin Framework: The Commitment Conversation

### Timing: When to Have It

**Too early:** After 1-2 dates (relax, you don't know each other yet)  
**Right time:** After 2-3 months of regular dating (you've established patterns)  
**Too late:** After 6+ months (why hasn't this been discussed?)

**Signal:** When you're thinking about them constantly and want to know they're thinking about you.

### How to Start It

**Don't:** "Where is this going?" (sounds accusatory)  
**Do:** "I'm really enjoying this. I want to know how you're feeling about us."

This is honest, vulnerable, and invites honesty back.

### What to Listen For

**Good signs:**
- They seem relieved you brought it up
- They give a clear answer
- They ask about your feelings
- They want the same thing as you

**Yellow flags:**
- They seem defensive
- They deflect ("Why are you asking?")
- They're vague ("Let's see where it goes")

**Red flags:**
- They know they don't want commitment but date you anyway
- They panic at the mention
- They're not ready to discuss it

### Possible Outcomes

**Outcome 1: You Both Want Exclusivity**  
Great. Make it official. Discuss what that means.

**Outcome 2: They're Not Sure**  
Ask: "When will you know?" and "What do you need to feel ready?"

Give them a timeframe (1-2 months). If they don't know by then, you have your answer: they're not interested in commitment with you.

**Outcome 3: They Don't Want Commitment**  
Believe them. Don't hope they'll change. Decide: Can you keep dating without commitment? If not, move on.

**Outcome 4: They Want More Than You're Ready For**  
Be honest. "I really like you, but I'm not ready for [marriage/living together/whatever]. I need more time."

## What Happens After the Conversation

**If you're exclusive:**
- Stop dating others (actually stop, not just stop telling them)
- Check in periodically ("I still like where this is going")
- Watch for warning signs (see red flags list)

**If they're unsure:**
- Don't settle into "waiting for clarity"
- Set a personal deadline (3 months)
- If clarity doesn't come, you have your answer

**If you broke up:**
- Grieve it (it's real, even if short)
- Don't stay friends immediately
- Move on cleanly

## The Hard Truth

Some people will panic at commitment talk. That tells you something important: **They're not ready for you.**

People who want you will want clarity with you.

## Key Takeaways

- Have the conversation after 2-3 months of regular dating
- Be honest and vulnerable (not accusatory)
- Listen for clarity and willingness
- Believe people when they tell you their capacity
- Don't stay in uncertainty hoping it resolves

## How Your Twin Guides You

Your Twin helps you see that asking for commitment isn't needy—it's self-respecting.

The right person will want to have this conversation with you.
